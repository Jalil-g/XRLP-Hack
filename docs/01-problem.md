## One-sentence version

Small builders can't get affordable construction credit, and the people who'd happily lend to them
can't see or control where their money goes once it leaves their hands.

## The market (how big is this?)

- **$453.5B** in acquisition, development & construction (AD&C) loans sat on FDIC-insured bank balance sheets in
  Q2 2026. **$91.3B** of that is 1–4 family residential construction, which is still **~55% below** the Q1 2008
  peak of $204B. Banks never returned to small-builder lending at the old scale.
  [NAHB Eye on Housing / FDIC data, Aug 31 2026](https://eyeonhousing.org/2026/08/single-family-adc-lending-falls-back-in-q2/)
- US private credit grew from ~$350B (2012) to **>$2.6T** in assets by end-2025 per a Fed FEDS note (reported
  secondhand, **unverified** against the note itself) [Central Banking](https://www.centralbanking.com/node/7977476).
  The NY + Dallas Fed put US direct lending alone at **>$1.3T** and launched a pilot survey because visibility is poor.
  [Dallas Fed, Aug 2026](https://www.dallasfed.org/news/releases/2026/nr260805privatecredit)
- Retail money is moving into private credit fast. Wellington projects US retail allocation will grow from
  ~$0.1T to **$2.4T by 2030**. That's a single asset manager's forecast.
  [Wellington 2026 outlook](https://www.wellington.com/en-ch/intermediary/insights/2026-private-credit-outlook)
- Real-estate crowdfunding is ~$10B globally (2025). Debt is **~62%** of it and North America is ~53%. These are
  market-research estimates, and other firms put it as high as $49B.
  [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/real-estate-crowdfunding-market)

## Pain 1 — Small builders are starved of construction credit

- NAHB's AD&C financing survey: Q2 2026 was the **18th consecutive quarter** in which builders and developers
  reported *tightening* credit (net easing index −12.0, down from −2.7 in Q1).
  [eyeonhousing.org, Aug 2026](https://eyeonhousing.org/2026/08/cost-of-credit-for-builders-up-since-the-end-of-2025/)
- Over the same quarter the Fed's lender survey read **+3.7 (easing)**. This is the **first time since 2013** that
  lenders said "easing" while builders said "tightening". Capital exists, but it isn't reaching small builders. (same source)
- How lenders tighten: **53%** of builders were asked for personal guarantees or collateral unrelated to the
  project, and **47%** each saw higher rates, lower LTV/LTC, or refused relationship loans. (same source)
- Effective rates in Q1 2026 ran **9.36% (land acquisition) to 11.68% (pre-sold single-family construction)**,
  and average initial points rose on all four AD&C loan categories in Q2 2026. [NAHB AD&C survey](https://www.nahb.org/news-and-economics/housing-economics/indices/adc-financing-survey)
- When banks say no, builders turn to private/hard-money lenders. HMDA data puts the 2025 median note rate on
  short-term investor loans at **9.99%** (10.25% at non-banks), and that's *before* points, which HMDA doesn't publish.
  [CrowdfundedWealth analysis of HMDA](https://www.crowdfundedwealth.com/articles/hard-money-loan-rates-2026).
  Lender-published ground-up ranges run **12–15%** plus 1–3 points, with **$150–250 per draw inspection**.
  [Cor Advisors](https://www.coradvisors.net/2026/08/hard-money-loans-vs-bridge-loans-2026.html),
  [Harbert Group](https://www.harbertgroup.com/blog/1051/Texas+Hard+Money+Loans+2026%3A+Houston+Fix-and-flip+Lender+Rates%2C+Terms%2C+And+Honest+Roi) (lender marketing)
- Small builders depend heavily on community banks for AD&C financing. When credit is costly they cut
  starts and inventory. [House Small Business Committee testimony, May 2026](https://democrats-smallbusiness.house.gov/UploadedFiles/05-21-26_Mr._Owens_Testimony.pdf)

## Pain 2 — Retail credit investors are flying blind

- **CrowdStreet / Nightingale (2023):** ~$63M raised from ~800 accredited investors on CrowdStreet was wired into
  an account controlled by the sponsor instead of escrow, and most of it was diverted to the CEO's accounts. The CEO
  was sentenced to 87 months, and reportedly only ~13% had been recovered (**unverified**, single source). CrowdStreet
  added mandatory third-party escrow only *after* the fraud.
  [Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/office/doj-sec-investigating-nightingale-after-45m-of-investor-cash-allegedly-diverted-to-ceos-accounts-120196),
  [Bisnow class action](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/capital-markets/crowdstreet-accused-of-raising-securities-without-a-license-in-class-action-128551),
  [AIN summary](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/capital-raising/crowdstreet-nightingale-fraud-2026) (secondary).
  **This is our single best story:** the money left the investors' control before anything was built.
- **Yieldstreet (now Willow Wealth):** CNBC's review of 30 real-estate deals found 4 total losses and 23 on a
  watchlist, with ~$78M of defaults over a year. In one case a $15M position was sold for **$1**. A further ~$41M
  of defaults were reported in Dec 2025.
  [Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/investment/yieldstreet-investors-report-massive-losses-from-failed-real-estate-bets-130607),
  [The Real Deal](https://therealdeal.com/national/2025/08/18/yieldstreet-real-estate-investments-implode),
  [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/alternatives/yieldstreet-now-willow-wealth-racks-up-more-losses-for-investors-report/263421)
- **Groundfloor** (Reg A+ real-estate debt, open to non-accredited investors): the company reports a ~0.85%
  historical loss rate [Groundfloor blog](https://blog.groundfloor.com/asset-management-monthly-april-25). A
  secondary review cites a **4.71% uncured default rate** from its FY2024 Form 1-K
  [AIN](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/real-estate/groundfloor-review-2026-real-estate-debt-crowdfunding-platform)
  (**unverified**, check EDGAR). Investors complain about defaults, slow resolution, and *reduced per-loan detail in
  the app* [Trustpilot](https://it.trustpilot.com/review/groundfloor.com) (anecdotal),
  [Finder review](https://finder.com/real-estate-investing/groundfloor-review).
- **On-chain private credit didn't fix this either.** Goldfinch put loans on-chain, but disbursement and
  monitoring stayed off-chain. One borrower lent $1.9M of a $5M loan to a subsidiary in breach of covenant, the
  defaults mounted, and the protocol wound down.
  [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/goldfinch-writes-down-failed-usd5m-loan-to-kenyan-taxi-financier),
  [The Defiant wind-down](https://thedefiant.io/news/defi/goldfinch-finance-winds-down-depositors-report-50m-defaults).
  Lesson: a public ledger of *loans* is not enough. You need on-chain control of *disbursement*.
- Today the investor trusts the *platform's* word that a draw was inspected and justified. There is no
  independent, tamper-evident record of who approved what, when, and based on what evidence.

## Pain 3 — The construction "draw" process is slow and fraud-prone

**Slow:**
- Draw turnaround runs **5–14 business days** from submission to funding, per Built's CEO (vendor)
  [Bisnow Studio B](https://www.bisnow.com/studio-b/national/construction-development/construction-project-operations-capital-built-technologies).
  In Rabbet's survey, lenders *think* draws take **5.5 business days**, but borrowers experience **9.4**
  (vendor; 2018 data) [Rabbet](https://rabbet.com/blog/draw-processing-why-lenders-and-borrowers-disagree).
- Built now says **inspections, not underwriting, are the bottleneck** for many lenders (vendor)
  [Built](https://getbuilt.com/blog/hybrid-construction-draw-inspections/). Other sources point the same way:
  [ICBA, Feb 2025](https://www.icba.org/w/mitigating-risk-in-construction-draw-loans),
  [Levelset](https://www.levelset.com/blog/construction-draw-inspection/).
- **70%** of 250 GCs/subs regularly face late payments. They inflate bids by **~8%** to cover slow payers, and
  **35%** have had projects cancelled or significantly delayed by financing gaps. (Talker Research for Built, May 2025, vendor)
  [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250521182315/en/Constructions-Cash-Crunch-70-of-Contractors-Say-Payment-Delays-Threaten-Industry)
- Rabbet's 2024 report: slow payments cost US construction **~$280B** in 2024. **82%** of contractors faced
  payment delays (up from 49% two years earlier), and **97%** of GCs raised bid prices to offset delays and financing
  costs. (vendor; methodology not published)
  [Rabbet](https://rabbet.com/blog/how-slow-payments-are-costing-the-construction-industry-billions)
- **92%** of subcontractors floated payroll from their own funds while waiting to be paid, and **59%** wait 46+ days
  for payment (Siteline survey of 492 pros, May 2026, vendor)
  [Siteline](https://www.siteline.com/blog/youre-not-behind-the-whole-industry-is-built-this-way)
- Builders are told to keep 5–10 days of cash float per draw, and 5–10% of each draw is commonly held
  back as retainage. [Projul draw guide](https://projul.com/blog/construction-loan-draw-guide/)

**Fraud-prone:**
- FinCEN's analysis of commercial-real-estate fraud SARs found that filings **almost tripled 2007→2010**.
  "Construction loan" was one of the most common terms in those SARs. **Nearly 30%** involved suspected
  misappropriation of funds, often discovered by "inspecting sites with little or no construction work performed".
  The typologies include falsified **draw requests, lien waivers, and invoices**, plus *"receipts dated prior to the
  loan"*, which is exactly the check our AI runs.
  [FinCEN CRE Financing Fraud report, 2011](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/shared/Commercial%20Real%20Estate%20Financing%20Fraud%20FINAL%20508.pdf),
  [Advisory FIN-2011-A007](https://www.fincen.gov/resources/advisories/fincen-advisory-fin-2011-a007)
  (old but authoritative. We found **no newer FinCEN construction-specific data**, so say so if asked.)
- Recent federal cases:
  - **Nebraska, Jul 2026:** a former bank CFO used inflated contractor invoices to draw **~$4.32M** of construction
    loan proceeds and got 36 months. [DOJ D. Neb.](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ne/pr/former-bank-cfo-sentenced-36-months-imprisonment-bank-fraud-scheme-involving-car-wash)
  - **Houston, 2025:** a custom home builder sent false invoices to the banks holding his clients' construction
    loans, and the draws landed in accounts he controlled. He got 51 months plus $2.3M restitution.
    [DOJ S.D. Tex.](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdtx/pr/houston-custom-home-builder-heads-prison-misusing-construction-funds)
  - **Anchorage, 2012:** 12 draw requests certified work that was barely done, and **$4.3M** was disbursed before
    default. [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/anchorage/press-releases/2012/real-estate-developer-admits-to-submitting-false-information-to-credit-union-to-obtain-4-million-in-construction-loan-funds/)
  - **Grand Junction, CO, 2013:** altered and *duplicate* invoices supported draws, followed by laundering of the proceeds.
    [DOJ D. Colo.](https://www.justice.gov/usao-co/pr/grand-junction-real-estate-developers-arrested-bank-fraud)
- Known pattern: **photos reused from earlier phases or other job sites** to claim progress.
  [Truepic](https://www.truepic.com/blog/construction-lending-risks) (vendor claim)

## Our wedge

Make the draw itself the trust mechanism, enforced on a public ledger:

1. Pooled investor money sits in an XRPL **Vault** and is lent through the native **Lending Protocol**.
2. Each milestone's money is locked in an XRPL **escrow** that can only go to *that contractor* or back
   to the project — **not even the platform can redirect it**. (This is the anti-Nightingale guarantee.)
3. Release needs a **human inspector** (who holds the escrow's release key) after an **independent AI evidence
   check**; both approvals are signed on-chain.
4. Every approval is signed and timestamped on-chain; anyone can audit it.
5. If a project stalls, escrows expire; unreleased money can only return to the project account, which repays the
   vault (multi-sig `LoanPay`), and investors redeem at the vault's redemption date. Not automatic, but nobody can
   divert it.

## Honest caveats (say these out loud in the pitch)

- Offering loan participations to the public is a regulated activity. This is a **prototype, not an offer to invest**.
  On-chain Credentials + Permissioned Domains show how we'd restrict participation to KYC'd / accredited investors.
- The AI checks that evidence is **consistent** (time, place, documents, budget); the human inspector confirms **quality**.
- Money already released to a builder is at risk if they default — that's lending (platform first-loss cover absorbs
  part of it). Unreleased money is protected: it can only go to the builder on approval or back toward the vault.
- The inspector holds the release key; the AI verdict is an on-chain attestation, not a ledger-enforced veto
  (enforced multi-party release needs Smart Escrows, not yet on devnet).
- Much of the draw-delay data comes from vendors who sell draw software (Built, Rabbet, Siteline). The
  independent anchors are NAHB/FDIC (credit), FinCEN/DOJ (fraud), and press coverage of Nightingale/Yieldstreet (investor harm).
- XLS-65/66 are **not on mainnet yet**. The LendingProtocolV1_1 validator vote opened ~Oct 1 2026, and the amendments
  need >80% of validators for 2 weeks. [Crypto Times](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/10/01/xrpl-opens-new-lending-protocol-amendment-for-validator-vote/),
  [CoinGape](https://coingape.com/xrp-ledger-new-lending-protocol-version-is-now-open-for-validator-voting/)
